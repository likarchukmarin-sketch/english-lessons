Context & discovery · Two plans

8 minutes · Read first. Notice the highlighted structure.

Anna: What are you going to do this weekend?

Lisa: On Saturday, I'm going to make candles. In the evening, I'm going to watch a series.

Anna: Nice! I'm going to visit my friend on Saturday. And on Sunday?

Lisa: I'm not going to work on Sunday. I'm going to read a book.

Anna: Are you going to go shopping, too?

Check your understanding

1. What is Lisa going to do on Saturday?
2. Is Lisa going to work on Sunday?
3. Is Lisa going to go shopping?
Think about the meaning

Does Lisa talk about her usual routine or her plan for the weekend?

Answer: A plan for the weekend. She has already decided what she wants to do.

Grammar focus · Be going to

10 minutes · Look at the pattern. You do not need to memorise everything at once.

We use be going to to talk about plans and intentions in the future.

1. Positive sentences

Subject + am / is / are + going to + verb
SubjectBeExample
IamI'm going to read.
He / She / ItisShe is going to work.
You / We / TheyareThey are going to travel.

2. Negative sentences

Subject + am / is / are + not + going to + verb

I'm not going to work. · She isn't going to cook. · We aren't going to travel.

3. Questions

Am / Is / Are + subject + going to + verb?

Are you going to read? — Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.

Is she going to work? — Yes, she is. / No, she isn't.

Quick reminder: will and going to

Will can be used for a decision made now or a prediction: “I think it will rain.”

Be going to is our focus today for a plan already decided: “I'm going to make candles on Saturday.”

For this lesson, focus on one clear use: plans → be going to. We are not studying all future forms today.

Build the sentence

Choose the correct form of be.

1. You ___ going to read tonight.
2. She ___ going to make candles.
3. I ___ not going to work on Sunday.
