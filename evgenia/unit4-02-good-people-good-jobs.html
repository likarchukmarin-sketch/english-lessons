1. Quick recall + People Map live

12 min
catch-up from Lesson 1

30-second recall — Lesson 1 chunks

Before you start, quickly say what each one means — don't check below until you've tried.

get on well with look up to stay in touch with take after rely on catch up with
get on well with — have a good relationship with · look up to — admire/respect · stay in touch with — keep contacting · take after — be similar to (a relative) · rely on — depend on · catch up with — meet after a while apart

Now say your People Map again — live, without your notes or the recording. Talk about your 4 people. This time, add at least one frequency adverb per person (always / usually / often / sometimes / rarely), and try to fit in 1–2 of the chunks above.

Teacher will ask follow-up questions like:

  • How often do you actually see this person?
  • What's something you always / never do together?
  • Is there anything different about them right now compared to usual?

Quick reminder — adverbs of frequency

alwaysusuallyoften sometimesrarely / hardly evernever

Position: before the main verb — I usually call her — but after "to be" — She's usually busy.

3. Grammar — verb patterns

8 min

This is grammar you already know passively — the goal is to notice the pattern and activate it faster.

Three patterns

1. Verb + to + infinitive: She decided to become a chef. He hopes to work abroad.
2. Verb + -ing: I enjoy talking to customers. He finished training last year.
3. Preposition + -ing: She's responsible for planning the menu. He's interested in helping people.

Spot the mistake

✗ After leaving school, I decided do a plumbing course.
✓ ...I decided to do a plumbing course.
✗ She hopes for become a chef.
✓ She hopes to become a chef.
✗ You need to speaking to a financial consultant.
✓ You need to speak to a financial consultant.

4. Guided practice

6 min
also finishing off adverbs of frequency

Complete these out loud about real jobs (yours, or someone you know). Try to include a frequency adverb in at least one.

I ___ (decide / want / hope) to ___ in my job.
I ___ (always / usually / sometimes) enjoy ___ -ing at work.
My ___ (job/colleague) is responsible for ___ -ing.

5. Speaking — talk about a job

10 min

Choose one real person you know (or yourself) and their job. Speak for about a minute without notes: what the job is, why they chose it, what they're responsible for, how often they do certain tasks, and one personality trait that helps them do it well. Your teacher will ask 2–3 follow-up questions.

Try to include:

  • at least one verb + to-infinitive (decided to.../wants to...)
  • at least one verb/preposition + -ing (enjoys.../responsible for...)
  • at least one frequency adverb (usually/often/rarely...)
  • at least one personality adjective from Lesson 1

7. Self-check

1 min
  • I can describe a job using verb patterns (decide to / enjoy -ing / responsible for -ing).
  • I can use frequency adverbs correctly in the right position.
  • I can describe my People Map live, without reading notes.
  • I can answer follow-up questions about work without long pauses.

8. Homework

1 min

Voice message, about 30–40 seconds

Talk about your own job (or a job you'd like to have). Use 3 new work-related words from today, at least one verb pattern (to-infinitive or -ing), and one frequency adverb.

Goal: keep building spontaneous retrieval without support — the recording is just for you to check your own fluency, not to be perfect.
