Section 03 · 8 minutes

Reading

Should Every Great Book Become a Film?

When a book meets the big screen
Before reading: Can a film ever be better than the book it's based on?

Imagine that you have just finished a brilliant novel. The plot was gripping, the characters felt like real people, and the ending stayed in your mind for days. But then you hear the news: a film adaptation is coming. At first, you are excited, but a small question appears: what if the film changes everything you loved?

Many book lovers feel nervous when their favourite stories move to the cinema. One reason is imagination. When we read, we create our own pictures of the characters, places and events. We decide what a mysterious house looks like or how heroes sound. A film gives us someone else's version. Even a talented director may imagine the story differently from us.

There is another problem: time. A novel can have hundreds of pages, but most films last around two hours. A screenwriter often has to remove characters, shorten conversations, or change parts of the plot. Sometimes an important chapter disappears. The original ending may also change because the director wants a more surprising or emotional finish. For readers, these changes can be disappointing. A story that felt complex on the page may become too simple on screen.

However, a film adaptation can also bring something wonderful. Talented actors can make familiar characters feel new. A beautiful soundtrack can create emotion, while visual effects can turn an imaginary world into something visually stunning. A film can also introduce a story to people who would never pick up the book, and sometimes a new version makes us want to read the original novel.

Of course, not every adaptation is successful. Some are predictable, while others miss the heart of the story. But a film doesn't have to be an exact copy to be good. Perhaps the best adaptations respect the main ideas and feelings of a book while finding a fresh way to tell the story.

So, should every great book become a film? Maybe not. Some stories are perfect in our imagination; others are waiting for the right actors, directors, and screenwriters. The real question may not be whether a film is better than the book, but whether it gives us another reason to love the story.

Check your understanding

1. Why can readers feel worried when their favourite book becomes a film?

2. What does a book allow readers to decide for themselves?

3. Why do screenwriters sometimes remove characters or change the ending?

4. How can a film bring something new to the original story?

5. According to the text, what makes a good adaptation?

Open suggested answers
1. Readers may worry that the film will change the things they loved about the book.
2. They can imagine the characters, places and events for themselves.
3. Films are shorter than novels, so screenwriters need to shorten the story; directors may also want a more surprising or emotional ending.
4. Actors, music and visual effects can bring characters and imaginary worlds to life. A film can also introduce the story to new people.
5. A good adaptation respects the book's main ideas and feelings while finding a fresh way to tell the story.

Your speaking project

You're the Film Director!

Create your own film adaptation.

A famous author has sold the rights to their best-selling novel. You are directing the film adaptation. You have a limited budget and only two hours to tell the story.

Your task

Choose a book (it can be a famous book or one you love) and prepare a short presentation about your film adaptation. Explain your decisions.

Make your decisions

  1. Would you keep the original ending or create a surprising new one?
  2. Would you keep all the characters or remove some?
  3. Would you choose famous actors or talented unknown actors?
  4. Would you use emotional music or a simple soundtrack?
  5. Would you follow the book closely or add new scenes?
  6. Would you show the best scenes in the trailer or keep the story mysterious?
  7. What would you call your film, and who would you want to watch it?

What to include

You can adapt a real book or invent a possible adaptation. Describe:

  • The book and its story.
  • The main characters and casting choices.
  • The changes you would make.
  • Your version of the ending.
  • The target audience and why people would enjoy the film.
Presentation length: 1–2 minutes.

Useful language

I'd cast ... as ...I'd keep/change ... because ...The story would be set in ...I'd remove ... to make the story shorter.My film would appeal to ...I think the audience would love it because ...Unlike the original book, my film would ...
