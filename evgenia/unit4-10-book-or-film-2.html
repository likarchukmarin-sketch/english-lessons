05 · Read, react, discuss

Should Every Great Book Become a Film?

10–12 minutes

Should Every Great Book Become a Film?

When a book meets the big screen

Imagine that you have just finished a brilliant novel. The plot was gripping, the characters felt like real people, and the ending stayed in your mind for days. Then you hear the news: a film adaptation is coming. At first, you are excited. But a small question appears: what if the film changes everything you loved?

Many book lovers feel nervous when their favourite stories move to the cinema. One reason is imagination. When we read, we create our own pictures of the characters, places and events. We decide what a mysterious house looks like or how a hero sounds. A film gives us someone else's version. Even a talented director may imagine the story differently from us.

There is another problem: time. A novel can have hundreds of pages, but most films last around two hours. A screenwriter often has to remove characters, shorten conversations or change parts of the plot. Sometimes an important chapter disappears. The original ending may also change because the director wants a more surprising or emotional finish. For readers, these changes can be disappointing. A story that felt complex on the page may become too simple on screen.

However, a film adaptation can also bring something wonderful. Talented actors can make familiar characters feel new. A beautiful soundtrack can create emotion, while visual effects can turn an imaginary world into something visually stunning. A film can also introduce a story to people who would never pick up the book. And sometimes a new version makes us want to read the original novel.

Of course, not every adaptation is successful. Some are predictable, while others miss the heart of the story. But a film does not have to be an exact copy to be good. Perhaps the best adaptations respect the main ideas and feelings of a book while finding a fresh way to tell the story.

So, should every great book become a film? Maybe not. Some stories are perfect in our imagination. Others are waiting for the right actors, director and screenwriter. The real question may not be whether a film is better than the book, but whether it gives us another reason to love the story.

Check your understanding

1. Why can readers feel worried when their favourite book becomes a film?
2. What does a book allow readers to decide for themselves?
3. Why do screenwriters sometimes remove characters or change the ending?
4. How can a film bring something new to the original story?
5. According to the text, what makes a good adaptation?

After reading: what do you think?

1. Do you agree that some stories are better left on the page? Why?
2. Is it a problem if a director changes the original ending?
3. Can a bad adaptation damage the reputation of a good book?
4. Should authors have the final say in how their books are adapted?

07 · Take it beyond the lesson

Homework: two creative missions

Mission 01

Your Personal Review

The review that makes us curious

Choose a book or film that made a strong impression on you. Prepare a 1.5–2 minute voice message OR 8–10 written sentences.

Answer these questions:

  1. What is it about?
  2. What makes it special?
  3. What did you like or dislike?
  4. Was it gripping, predictable, hilarious or thought-provoking?
  5. Would you recommend it? Why?
  6. Has it changed the way you think about something?

Mission rule: Use at least FIVE expressions from the lesson vocabulary.

Mission 02

You're the Film Director!

Create your own film adaptation

A famous author has sold the rights to their bestselling novel. You are directing the adaptation. You have a limited budget and only two hours to tell the story.

Make six decisions and explain each one:

  1. Keep the original ending or create a surprising new one?
  2. Keep all characters or remove some?
  3. Choose famous actors or talented unknown actors?
  4. Use emotional music or a simple soundtrack?
  5. Follow the book or add new scenes?
  6. Show the best scenes in the trailer or keep it mysterious?

Mission rule: Prepare six decisions and reasons. Be ready to defend your choices next lesson.
